# scfp1 robustness experiment

Generated by `tests/experiment` (make_dataset.py + analyze.js). Photos: 22 distinct 4000x3000 JPEGs (q95, simulated sensor noise; 5 stored sideways with EXIF orientation 6, like portrait phone photos). Copies scored: 858. Fingerprints computed with securecam-core.js; the NumPy port used to generate them was verified byte-identical on 9 images.

Source photos are Windows wallpapers (landscapes, cities, abstract theme art), including sets of colour variants of the same picture — a hard case for a brightness-based fingerprint.

## Same file, phone decode vs full decode

The phone fingerprints a reduced-size decode (like Android's inSampleSize); a verifier decodes at full size. Max pHash difference 2 bits, max tile difference luma 4.72 / chroma 1.13.

## Shipped defaults (securecam-core.js)

lookup ≤12 bits or 2 quadrants ≤6; tile luma 9, chroma 7.

The tile thresholds are deliberately above the best sweep row: in an earlier run that still included Windows' img0 wallpaper (very sharp high-contrast edges), clean screenshot/Instagram copies reached a tile luma difference of 7.65. A threshold of 9 keeps a safety margin for such photos at a cost of well under 1% edit detection.

## Known limits

- **Blur and smoothing** of a region keep its average colour, which is what the fingerprint records, so they are not detected by the visual check. A per-region texture measure was prototyped and rejected: recompression alone changed regional texture more than a real blur did. The exact byte check still catches any blur on an original file.
- **Very small edits** (well under 1% of the photo) and edits that keep the average colour of the area (a flat fill with the area's own median colour) can pass.
- **Crops and aspect-ratio changes** (e.g. Instagram 4:5) change the grid; such copies are reported as UNVERIFIED, not as authentic.
- Test photos are wallpapers with simulated sensor noise, not real phone captures. Repeat the run with real SecureCam photos (see README) before quoting final numbers.
